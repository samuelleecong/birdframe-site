Canon EOS 5D Mark IV for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the Canon EOS 5D Mark IV puts on a bird, worked out from its sensor and the lenses people pair with it: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: the Canon EOS 5D Mark IV records 6,720 × 4,480 pixels (30.1 megapixels) on a full-frame sensor of 36.0 × 24.0 mm, so each pixel is 5.4 µm across. With a 400 mm lens that puts about 728 pixels across an American robin at 25 m: crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 36% of the width. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

How the 5D Mark IV's 30.1 megapixels compare

On the same lens, reach comes down to how many pixels fit in each millimetre of sensor. The 5D Mark IV packs 187 pixels into each millimetre of its 36.0 mm-wide full-frame sensor; the 24-megapixel Canon EOS R6 Mark II has 167 and the 45-megapixel Nikon Z8 230. On the same lens, at the same distance, the 5D Mark IV puts 1.12 times as many pixels across the bird as the R6 Mark II: 728 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 400 mm, against 652.

Questions

How many pixels does the Canon EOS 5D Mark IV put on a bird?

It depends on the lens and the distance. With 400 mm, about 728 pixels across an American robin at 25 m and 361 at 50 m; with 600 mm, 1,102 and 544. The sum: the bird's length × focal length ÷ (distance − focal length), divided by the pixel pitch (5.4 µm).

How far can you crop a 5D Mark IV photo for iNaturalist or a contest?

A horizontal crop has to keep 30% of the frame's width to fill iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels, 45% for Audubon's 3,000-pixel entries and 52% for Australian Geographic's 3,500-pixel finalist files (2026 rules). Crop from the full-size original and never enlarge a crop to reach a size.

Does the 5D Mark IV give more reach than a 24-megapixel full-frame camera?

A little: the 5D Mark IV has 187 pixels per millimetre of sensor and the Canon EOS R6 Mark II 167, so on the same lens and distance the 5D Mark IV puts 1.12 times as many pixels across the bird. The 45-megapixel Nikon Z8 has 230, so it puts 1.23 times as many as the 5D Mark IV.

What shutter speed freezes a bird in flight with the 5D Mark IV?

With an osprey at about 29 m on 400 mm, wings across two thirds of the frame, the wingtips blur about 19 pixels at 1/2000 and 9 at 1/4000 mid-stroke when you pan with the bird. Holding them within 3 pixels would take about 1/12800, so a burst is what catches the wings sharp near the top or bottom of the stroke. Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates.
