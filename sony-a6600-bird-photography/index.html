Sony A6600 for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the Sony A6600 puts on a bird, worked out from its sensor and the lenses people pair with it: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: the Sony A6600 records 6,000 × 4,000 pixels (24.0 megapixels) on an APS-C sensor of 23.5 × 15.6 mm, so each pixel is 3.9 µm across. With a 600 mm lens that puts about 1,507 pixels across an American robin at 25 m: crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 74% of the width. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

The Sony A6600 has the same sensor size and pixel count as the Sony A6400, so every figure here holds for the A6400 too (A6400 guide).

Why the A6600's small pixels matter

On the same lens, reach comes down to how many pixels fit in each millimetre of sensor. The A6600 packs 255 pixels into each millimetre of its 23.5 mm-wide APS-C sensor; the full-frame Canon EOS R6 Mark II has 167. On the same lens, at the same distance, the A6600 puts 1.53 times as many pixels across the bird: 1,507 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 600 mm, against 986 on the R6 Mark II.

Holding the osprey's wingtips within 3 pixels at this distance would take about 1/12800; for the bald eagle, about 1/10000. The wings slow to a stop at the top and bottom of each stroke, which is why a burst catches sharp wingtips that a single frame usually misses.

Questions

How many pixels does the Sony A6600 put on a bird?

It depends on the lens and the distance. With 600 mm, about 1,507 pixels across an American robin at 25 m and 744 at 50 m; with 800 mm, 2,026 and 996. The sum: the bird's length × focal length ÷ (distance − focal length), divided by the pixel pitch (3.9 µm).

How far can you crop an A6600 photo for iNaturalist or a contest?

A horizontal crop has to keep 34% of the frame's width to fill iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels, 50% for Audubon's 3,000-pixel entries and 58% for Australian Geographic's 3,500-pixel finalist files (2026 rules). Crop from the full-size original and never enlarge a crop to reach a size.

Does the A6600's APS-C sensor give more reach than a full-frame camera?

Against a 24-megapixel full-frame body, yes: the A6600 has 255 pixels per millimetre of sensor and the Canon EOS R6 Mark II 167, so on the same lens and distance the A6600 puts 1.53 times as many pixels across the bird. The 45-megapixel Nikon Z8 has 230, so the A6600's lead over it is 1.11 times, but Sony's full-frame A7R V (266) and A7R VI (278) put a little more on the bird (1.04 and 1.09 times as many pixels).

What shutter speed freezes a bird in flight with the A6600?

With an osprey at about 65 m on 600 mm, wings across two thirds of the frame, the wingtips blur about 17 pixels at 1/2000 and 9 at 1/4000 mid-stroke when you pan with the bird. Holding them within 3 pixels would take about 1/12800, so a burst is what catches the wings sharp near the top or bottom of the stroke. Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates.
