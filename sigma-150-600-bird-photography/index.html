Sigma 150-600mm for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the Sigma 150–600 mm puts on a bird at 600 mm, worked out from the focal length and the sensors of six popular bodies: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: at 600 mm the Sigma 150-600 puts about 1,568 pixels across an American robin at 25 m on the Sony A6700 (APS-C) and 986 on the Canon EOS R6 Mark II (full-frame). On the Sony A6700, crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 77% of the width. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

What the camera body changes

The lens puts the same image of the bird on every sensor: at 600 mm, an American robin at 25 m is 5.90 mm long on the sensor. The body decides how many pixels cover that image. In pixels per millimetre of sensor: Sony A6700 266, Canon EOS R7 312, Nikon D500 237, Sony A7 IV 195, Canon EOS R6 Mark II 167, Nikon Z8 230. So on the same lens, at the same distance, the Canon EOS R7 puts 1.87 times as many pixels on the bird as the Canon EOS R6 Mark II: 1,842 against 986 across that robin. The APS-C Nikon D500 and the full-frame Nikon Z8 have pixels of almost the same size (4.22 and 4.35 µm), so they put almost the same pixels on the bird (1,398 and 1,357 across that robin); the Nikon Z8 records a wider frame around it. A smaller sensor adds pixels on the bird only when its pixels are smaller (crop sensor vs full frame).

Which Sigma 150–600 mm these numbers fit

Any version, at 600 mm. The pixels on the bird depend only on the focal length, the distance and the camera's sensor, so the table holds for any version of the lens zoomed to 600 mm. It doesn't say which bodies each version fits: Sigma’s current version, the 150–600mm F5–6.3 DG DN OS | Sports, is made for L-Mount and Sony E-mount, and Sigma lists its TC-1411 (1.4x) and TC-2011 (2x) teleconverters for the L-Mount version only.

Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates (est.), so treat these as close guides rather than exact figures. Holding the osprey's wingtips within 3 pixels at this distance would take about 1/12800; for the bald eagle, about 1/10000. The wings slow to a stop at the top and bottom of each stroke, which is why a burst catches sharp wingtips that a single frame usually misses.

Is the Sigma 150-600 long enough for bird photography?

It depends on the bird and the distance. At 600 mm it puts about 1,568 pixels across an American robin at 25 m on the Sony A6700 and 775 at 50 m; a black-capped chickadee at 25 m gets 882. On the Canon EOS R6 Mark II the same robin at 25 m gets 986. Crop the Sony A6700 frame to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 77% of the width.

Does an APS-C body give the Sigma 150-600 more reach?
