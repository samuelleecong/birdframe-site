Best Nikon camera for bird photography? 10 bodies compared by pixels on the bird

Which Nikon camera puts the most pixels on a bird? Every Nikon body with a guide on this site, on the same 600 mm lens, at the same distances, so the only thing that changes is the sensor.

Short answer: on the same lens, the Nikon D5600 puts the most pixels on a bird: about 1,507 across an American robin at 25 m with 600 mm, against 994 on the Nikon Z5II, Nikon Z6III and Nikon Zf. This table measures reach only; autofocus, burst rate and handling matter too and aren't measured here. Each body's guide has its full numbers, from crop headroom to wingtip blur in flight.

What the table shows

On the same lens at the same distance, the bird's image on the sensor is the same size on every body, so reach comes down to how many pixels fit across it: the pixel pitch. The Nikon D5600 has the smallest pixels here (3.92 µm), so it puts 1.52 times as many pixels across the bird as the Nikon Z5II, Nikon Z6III and Nikon Zf (5.94 µm): 1,507 against 994 across an American robin at 25 m.

Megapixels alone don't measure reach. The 20.7-megapixel Nikon D500 puts more pixels on the bird than the 45.4-megapixel Nikon D850 (1,398 against 1,357 across the robin), because its pixels are smaller (4.22 µm against 4.35).

A full-frame body's DX crop mode doesn't add reach: it records the middle of the frame, so the bird fills more of a smaller picture with exactly the same pixels across it.

Bodies that share a sensor size and pixel count put exactly the same pixels on the bird with the same lens: the D500, D7500 and Z50II (23.5 mm and 5,568 pixels across); the D850, Z8 and Z9 (35.9 mm and 8,256 pixels across); the Z5II, Z6III and Zf (35.9 mm and 6,048 pixels across).

Across all 39 interchangeable-lens bodies with a guide on this site, on the same 600 mm lens, 16 bodies from other makers put more pixels on the bird than the Nikon D5600, led by the Panasonic Lumix G9 II with 3.00 µm pixels (1,970 across the robin at 25 m), and the Sony A6400 and Sony A6600 tie with it (same sensor size and pixel count).

Nikon's Coolpix P1000 and Coolpix P950 have built-in zooms (3,000 and 2,000 mm in 35mm terms, 539 and 357 mm real), so they can't take the same lens; they are compared at full zoom on the bridge camera page.

Questions

Which Nikon camera puts the most pixels on a bird?

On the same lens, the Nikon D5600 (3.92 µm pixels): about 1,507 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with a 600 mm lens. The Nikon Z5II, Nikon Z6III and Nikon Zf put 994 across the same robin, so the D5600 has 1.52 times as many. That's reach only; autofocus, burst rate and handling aren't measured here.

Do more megapixels mean more reach?

Not by themselves. On the same lens, reach comes down to pixel pitch: the 20.7-megapixel Nikon D500 (4.22 µm) puts 1,398 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 600 mm, and the 45.4-megapixel Nikon D850 (4.35 µm) 1,357.

Does a DX Nikon give more reach than full frame?

On the same lens, yes, but because of pixel size, not sensor size. Every DX Nikon body here has smaller pixels than every full-frame one, so each puts more pixels across the bird: 1,398 to 1,507 across an American robin at 25 m with 600 mm, against 994 to 1,357 on the full-frame bodies. A full-frame body records more of the scene around the bird.

Does a full-frame Nikon's DX crop mode add reach?

No. It records the middle of the frame, so the bird looks bigger in a smaller picture, but it gets exactly the same pixels. The D850 guide shows it: 1,126 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 500 mm, in the full frame or in DX crop mode.
