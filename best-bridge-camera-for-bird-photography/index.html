Best bridge camera for bird photography? 3 cameras compared by pixels on the bird

Which bridge camera puts the most pixels on a bird? Every bridge camera with a guide on this site, each at its own full zoom, against a robin and a chickadee at 10, 25 and 50 m, with a mirrorless camera and a real 600 mm lens for comparison.

Short answer: at full zoom the Nikon Coolpix P1000 puts the most pixels on a bird: about 3,950 across an American robin at 25 m, against 2,597 on the Nikon Coolpix P950 and 883 on the Sony RX10 IV. Those are pixels, not detail, and a bridge camera's zoom is quoted in 35mm terms: the RX10 IV's 600 mm is really 220 mm. This table measures reach only; autofocus, burst rate and handling matter too and aren't measured here.

“Won't fit” means the bird is longer than the frame is wide at that distance: zoom out. The sum, as in every guide: the bird's length × real focal length ÷ (distance − focal length) gives its size on the sensor, divided by the pixel pitch. The reach calculator and the shutter-speed calculator divide a bridge camera's 35mm-equivalent focal length by its 35mm-equivalent factor (5.566, 5.602 and 2.727 for the P1000, P950 and RX10 IV) to get the real one.

Read the zoom figure with care

A bridge camera's zoom is quoted in 35mm terms, not as its real focal length. The Sony RX10 IV's 600 mm is really 220 mm, so it puts 883 pixels across an American robin at 25 m, against 1,507 on a Sony A6400 with a real 600 mm lens. Its guide works out that its smaller lens opening also gathers less light and that its pixels hold less detail.

The Nikon Coolpix P1000 and Nikon Coolpix P950 put more pixels on the robin at 25 m than the A6400 with a 600 mm lens (3,950 and 2,597 against 1,507), because their pixels are tiny (1.34 µm) and their zooms are long (539 mm and 357 mm real). More pixels aren't more detail, though. At full zoom, diffraction alone spreads each point of light into a blur spot the width of about 8 pixels on the P1000 at f/8 and about 7 on the P950 at f/6.5, so neighbouring pixels can't hold separate detail. Their guides work it out for a robin at 50 m against a Nikon Z8 with a 600 mm lens: even with a perfect lens, the P1000's 1,953 pixels across it hold roughly the detail of the Z8's 670 and the P950's 1,289 pixels across it hold less detail than the Z8's 670. (Detail here is the finest pattern recorded at half contrast, MTF50.) Use these pixel counts for sizes and contest minimums, not as a promise of feather detail.

Close birds are the other catch: at full zoom an American robin at 10 m is longer than the frame is wide on the P1000 and the P950, so you zoom out, while the RX10 IV still fits it.

Questions

Which bridge camera puts the most pixels on a bird?

At full zoom, the Nikon Coolpix P1000: about 3,950 pixels across an American robin at 25 m, against 2,597 on the Nikon Coolpix P950 and 883 on the Sony RX10 IV. Those are pixels, not detail: at full zoom diffraction limits how fine the feathers can look.

Is a bridge camera's 600 mm the same as a 600 mm lens?

No. Bridge zooms are quoted in 35mm terms: the Sony RX10 IV's 600 mm is really 220 mm, so it puts about 883 pixels across an American robin at 25 m, against 1,507 on a Sony A6400 with a real 600 mm lens.

Can a bridge camera at full zoom fit a close bird?

Not always. At full zoom an American robin at 10 m is longer than the frame is wide on the Nikon Coolpix P1000 and the Nikon Coolpix P950, so zoom out; the Sony RX10 IV still fits it, with 2,238 pixels across the bird.
