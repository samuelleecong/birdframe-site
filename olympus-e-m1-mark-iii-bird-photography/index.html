Olympus OM-D E-M1 Mark III for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the Olympus OM-D E-M1 Mark III puts on a bird, worked out from its sensor and the lenses people pair with it: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: the Olympus OM-D E-M1 Mark III records 5,184 × 3,888 pixels (20.2 megapixels) on a Micro Four Thirds sensor of 17.4 × 13.0 mm, so each pixel is 3.4 µm across. With a 400 mm lens that puts about 1,163 pixels across an American robin at 25 m: crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 57% of the width. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

The Olympus OM-D E-M1 Mark III has the same sensor size and pixel count as the OM System OM-1 Mark II, OM System OM-1 and Olympus OM-D E-M1X, so every figure here holds for those bodies too (OM-1 Mark II guide, OM-1 guide, E-M1X guide).

Why the E-M1 Mark III's small pixels matter

On the same lens, reach comes down to how many pixels fit in each millimetre of sensor. The E-M1 Mark III packs 298 pixels into each millimetre of its 17.4 mm-wide Micro Four Thirds sensor; the full-frame Canon EOS R6 Mark II has 167. On the same lens, at the same distance, the E-M1 Mark III puts 1.78 times as many pixels across the bird: 1,163 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 400 mm, against 652 on the R6 Mark II.

Questions

How many pixels does the Olympus OM-D E-M1 Mark III put on a bird?

It depends on the lens and the distance. With 400 mm, about 1,163 pixels across an American robin at 25 m and 577 at 50 m; with 600 mm, 1,758 and 868. The sum: the bird's length × focal length ÷ (distance − focal length), divided by the pixel pitch (3.4 µm).

How far can you crop an E-M1 Mark III photo for iNaturalist or a contest?

A horizontal crop has to keep 40% of the frame's width to fill iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels, 58% for Audubon's 3,000-pixel entries and 68% for Australian Geographic's 3,500-pixel finalist files (2026 rules). Crop from the full-size original and never enlarge a crop to reach a size.

Does the E-M1 Mark III's Micro Four Thirds sensor give more reach than a full-frame camera?

Yes, against a 24-megapixel full-frame body: the E-M1 Mark III has 298 pixels per millimetre of sensor and the Canon EOS R6 Mark II 167, so on the same lens and distance the E-M1 Mark III puts 1.78 times as many pixels across the bird. The 45-megapixel Nikon Z8 has 230, so the E-M1 Mark III's lead over it is 1.30 times.

What shutter speed freezes a bird in flight with the E-M1 Mark III?

With an osprey at about 59 m on 400 mm, wings across two thirds of the frame, the wingtips blur about 15 pixels at 1/2000 and 7 at 1/4000 mid-stroke when you pan with the bird. Holding them within 3 pixels would take about 1/10000, so a burst is what catches the wings sharp near the top or bottom of the stroke. Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates.
