Canon EOS 7D Mark II for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the Canon EOS 7D Mark II puts on a bird, worked out from its sensor and the lenses people pair with it: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: the Canon EOS 7D Mark II records 5,472 × 3,648 pixels (20.0 megapixels) on an APS-C sensor of 22.4 × 15.0 mm, so each pixel is 4.1 µm across. With a 400 mm lens that puts about 953 pixels across an American robin at 25 m: crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 47% of the width. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

Why the 7D Mark II's small pixels matter

On the same lens, reach comes down to how many pixels fit in each millimetre of sensor. The 7D Mark II packs 244 pixels into each millimetre of its 22.4 mm-wide APS-C sensor; the full-frame Canon EOS R6 Mark II has 167. On the same lens, at the same distance, the 7D Mark II puts 1.46 times as many pixels across the bird: 953 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 400 mm, against 652 on the R6 Mark II.

The wings slow to a stop at the top and bottom of each stroke, which is why a burst catches sharp wingtips that a single frame usually misses.

Questions

How many pixels does the Canon EOS 7D Mark II put on a bird?

It depends on the lens and the distance. With 400 mm, about 953 pixels across an American robin at 25 m and 473 at 50 m; with 600 mm, 1,442 and 712. The sum: the bird's length × focal length ÷ (distance − focal length), divided by the pixel pitch (4.1 µm).

How far can you crop a 7D Mark II photo for iNaturalist or a contest?

A horizontal crop has to keep 37% of the frame's width to fill iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels, 55% for Audubon's 3,000-pixel entries and 64% for Australian Geographic's 3,500-pixel finalist files (2026 rules). Crop from the full-size original and never enlarge a crop to reach a size.

Does the 7D Mark II's APS-C sensor give more reach than a full-frame camera?

Yes, against a 24-megapixel full-frame body: the 7D Mark II has 244 pixels per millimetre of sensor and the Canon EOS R6 Mark II 167, so on the same lens and distance the 7D Mark II puts 1.46 times as many pixels across the bird. The 45-megapixel Nikon Z8 has 230, so the 7D Mark II's lead over it is 1.06 times.

What shutter speed freezes a bird in flight with the 7D Mark II?

With an osprey at about 46 m on 400 mm, wings across two thirds of the frame, the wingtips blur about 16 pixels at 1/2000 and 8 at 1/4000 mid-stroke when you pan with the bird. Holding them within 3 pixels would take about 1/12800, so a burst is what catches the wings sharp near the top or bottom of the stroke. Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates.
