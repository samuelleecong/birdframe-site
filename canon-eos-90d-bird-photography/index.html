Canon EOS 90D for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the Canon EOS 90D puts on a bird, worked out from its sensor and the lenses people pair with it: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: the Canon EOS 90D records 6,960 × 4,640 pixels (32.3 megapixels) on an APS-C sensor of 22.3 × 14.8 mm, so each pixel is 3.2 µm across. With a 400 mm lens that puts about 1,218 pixels across an American robin at 25 m: crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 59% of the width. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

The Canon EOS 90D has the same sensor size and pixel count as the Canon EOS R7, so every figure here holds for the R7 too (R7 guide).

Why the 90D's small pixels matter

On the same lens, reach comes down to how many pixels fit in each millimetre of sensor. The 90D packs 312 pixels into each millimetre of its 22.3 mm-wide APS-C sensor; the full-frame Canon EOS R6 Mark II has 167. On the same lens, at the same distance, the 90D puts 1.87 times as many pixels across the bird: 1,218 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 400 mm, against 652 on the R6 Mark II.

Holding the osprey's wingtips within 3 pixels at this distance would take about 1/16000; for the bald eagle, about 1/12800. The wings slow to a stop at the top and bottom of each stroke, which is why a burst catches sharp wingtips that a single frame usually misses.

Questions

How many pixels does the Canon EOS 90D put on a bird?

It depends on the lens and the distance. With 400 mm, about 1,218 pixels across an American robin at 25 m and 604 at 50 m; with 600 mm, 1,842 and 910. The sum: the bird's length × focal length ÷ (distance − focal length), divided by the pixel pitch (3.2 µm).

How far can you crop a 90D photo for iNaturalist or a contest?

A horizontal crop has to keep 29% of the frame's width to fill iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels, 43% for Audubon's 3,000-pixel entries and 50% for Australian Geographic's 3,500-pixel finalist files (2026 rules). Crop from the full-size original and never enlarge a crop to reach a size.

Does the 90D's APS-C sensor give more reach than a full-frame camera?

Yes, against a 24-megapixel full-frame body: the 90D has 312 pixels per millimetre of sensor and the Canon EOS R6 Mark II 167, so on the same lens and distance the 90D puts 1.87 times as many pixels across the bird. The 45-megapixel Nikon Z8 has 230, so the 90D's lead over it is 1.36 times.

What shutter speed freezes a bird in flight with the 90D?

With an osprey at about 46 m on 400 mm, wings across two thirds of the frame, the wingtips blur about 20 pixels at 1/2000 and 10 at 1/4000 mid-stroke when you pan with the bird. Holding them within 3 pixels would take about 1/16000, so a burst is what catches the wings sharp near the top or bottom of the stroke. Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates.
