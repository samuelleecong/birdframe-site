The best bird photo editing software for Mac, by job

There's no single best app for bird photos, because there's no single job. After an outing you pick the keepers, crop them, then edit the ones that matter. Each step has tools that are clearly better at it than the rest.

We make BirdFrame, one of the apps below. We list it only under the job it does. Other tools' details come from their makers' own pages and the Mac App Store, read on 27 October 2026; we haven't tested every one ourselves. Prices change, so check before you buy.

Short answer:

  • Cull: SuperPicky (free) or BurstPick ($89 once); see culling software compared.
  • Crop a whole set: BirdFrame (free for 50 photos per run, $29.99 once).
  • Edit RAW files: Lightroom Classic (subscription) or DxO PhotoLab (one-time).
  • Simple edits for free: Apple Photos.

1. Culling: picking the keepers

Bird photography means bursts, and most frames are near-duplicates. Culling tools that look at the bird itself save the most time.

2. Cropping: framing every bird

Most bird photos need a crop, and the bird is somewhere different in every frame. General editors crop one photo at a time, or copy the same box to every photo (Lightroom, darktable). Apple Photos can't copy a crop at all (details). Tools that auto-crop in batches, such as Aftershoot, Imagen and Capture One's AI Crop, are aimed mainly at portrait, wedding and event work. We couldn't find their crop features documented for birds, and none makes a separate crop of each subject.

4. Noise and sharpening

Lightroom Classic and DxO PhotoLab both have strong noise reduction built in. Topaz Photo (subscription) adds denoise, sharpening and upscaling. Be careful with upscaling if you submit records: eBird can tag artificially upscaled images as "Over-edited", and iNaturalist doesn't accept AI upscaling or generative fill. A crop from the original, plus light noise reduction, is accepted on both (eBird's rules, iNaturalist's).

Example setups

  • Free: SuperPicky to cull, BirdFrame's free tier to crop (50 photos per run), Apple Photos to edit.
  • No subscription: BurstPick or RobinRAW to cull, BirdFrame to crop, DxO PhotoLab to edit the best.
  • Adobe: Lightroom Classic to cull and edit, BirdFrame for cropping whole sets, especially for eBird and iNaturalist.

Questions

What software do bird photographers use on a Mac?

Usually two or three apps: one to cull (SuperPicky, BurstPick, RobinRAW or Lightroom's Assisted Culling), one to edit RAW files (Lightroom Classic, DxO PhotoLab, Capture One, or Apple Photos for simple edits), and sometimes one to crop a whole set at once (BirdFrame).

Is there free bird photo editing software for Mac?

Yes. Apple Photos and darktable are free editors, SuperPicky and Project Kestrel are free culling tools, and BirdFrame crops up to 50 photos per run for free.

Do I need a subscription?

No. DxO PhotoLab, BurstPick, RobinRAW, Excire Foto and BirdFrame are one-time purchases, and Capture One offers a perpetual licence. Lightroom, Aftershoot and Topaz Photo are subscriptions.

Is AI upscaling allowed on eBird or iNaturalist?

No. eBird can tag artificially upscaled images as Over-edited, and iNaturalist doesn't accept AI upscaling or generative fill. Cropping from the original is fine on both.
