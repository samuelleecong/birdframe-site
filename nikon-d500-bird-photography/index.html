Nikon D500 for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the Nikon D500 puts on a bird, worked out from its sensor and the lenses people pair with it: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: the Nikon D500 records 5,568 × 3,712 pixels (20.7 megapixels) on an APS-C sensor of 23.5 × 15.7 mm, so each pixel is 4.2 µm across. With a 500 mm lens that puts about 1,161 pixels across an American robin at 25 m: crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 57% of the width. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

The Nikon D500 has the same sensor size and pixel count as the Nikon Z50II, so every figure here holds for the Z50II too (Z50II guide).

Why the D500's small pixels matter

On the same lens, reach comes down to how many pixels fit in each millimetre of sensor. The D500 packs 237 pixels into each millimetre of its 23.5 mm-wide APS-C sensor; the full-frame Canon EOS R6 Mark II has 167. On the same lens, at the same distance, the D500 puts 1.42 times as many pixels across the bird: 1,161 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 500 mm, against 819 on the R6 Mark II.

Crop tighter and the file drops below the size; enlarging it to reach the number adds no detail, and the contests that ask for full-size files say so (crop rules and minimum sizes for 11 contests). Holding the osprey's wingtips within 3 pixels at this distance would take about 1/12800; for the bald eagle, about 1/10000. The wings slow to a stop at the top and bottom of each stroke, which is why a burst catches sharp wingtips that a single frame usually misses.

Questions

How many pixels does the Nikon D500 put on a bird?

It depends on the lens and the distance. With 500 mm, about 1,161 pixels across an American robin at 25 m and 574 at 50 m; with 600 mm, 1,398 and 691. The sum: the bird's length × focal length ÷ (distance − focal length), divided by the pixel pitch (4.2 µm).

How far can you crop a D500 photo for iNaturalist or a contest?

A horizontal crop has to keep 37% of the frame's width to fill iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels, 54% for Audubon's 3,000-pixel entries and 63% for Australian Geographic's 3,500-pixel finalist files (2026 rules). Crop from the full-size original and never enlarge a crop to reach a size.

Does the D500's APS-C sensor give more reach than a full-frame camera?

Yes, against a 24-megapixel full-frame body: the D500 has 237 pixels per millimetre of sensor and the Canon EOS R6 Mark II 167, so on the same lens and distance the D500 puts 1.42 times as many pixels across the bird. The 45-megapixel Nikon Z8 has 230, so the D500's lead over it is 1.03 times.

What shutter speed freezes a bird in flight with the D500?

With an osprey at about 55 m on 500 mm, wings across two thirds of the frame, the wingtips blur about 16 pixels at 1/2000 and 8 at 1/4000 mid-stroke when you pan with the bird. Holding them within 3 pixels would take about 1/12800, so a burst is what catches the wings sharp near the top or bottom of the stroke. Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates.
