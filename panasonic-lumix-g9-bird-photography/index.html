Panasonic Lumix G9 for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the Panasonic Lumix G9 puts on a bird, worked out from its sensor and the lenses people pair with it: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: the Panasonic Lumix G9 records 5,184 × 3,888 pixels (20.2 megapixels) on a Micro Four Thirds sensor of 17.3 × 13.0 mm, so each pixel is 3.3 µm across. With a 400 mm lens that puts about 1,169 pixels across an American robin at 25 m: crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 57% of the width. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

Why the G9's small pixels matter

On the same lens, reach comes down to how many pixels fit in each millimetre of sensor. The G9 packs 300 pixels into each millimetre of its 17.3 mm-wide Micro Four Thirds sensor; the full-frame Canon EOS R6 Mark II has 167. On the same lens, at the same distance, the G9 puts 1.79 times as many pixels across the bird: 1,169 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 400 mm, against 652 on the R6 Mark II.

Questions

How many pixels does the Panasonic Lumix G9 put on a bird?

It depends on the lens and the distance. With 400 mm, about 1,169 pixels across an American robin at 25 m and 580 at 50 m; with 600 mm, 1,768 and 873. The sum: the bird's length × focal length ÷ (distance − focal length), divided by the pixel pitch (3.3 µm).

How far can you crop a G9 photo for iNaturalist or a contest?

A horizontal crop has to keep 40% of the frame's width to fill iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels, 58% for Audubon's 3,000-pixel entries and 68% for Australian Geographic's 3,500-pixel finalist files (2026 rules). Crop from the full-size original and never enlarge a crop to reach a size.

Does the G9's Micro Four Thirds sensor give more reach than a full-frame camera?

Yes, against a 24-megapixel full-frame body: the G9 has 300 pixels per millimetre of sensor and the Canon EOS R6 Mark II 167, so on the same lens and distance the G9 puts 1.79 times as many pixels across the bird. The 45-megapixel Nikon Z8 has 230, so the G9's lead over it is 1.30 times.

What shutter speed freezes a bird in flight with the G9?

With an osprey at about 59 m on 400 mm, wings across two thirds of the frame, the wingtips blur about 15 pixels at 1/2000 and 7 at 1/4000 mid-stroke when you pan with the bird. Holding them within 3 pixels would take about 1/10000, so a burst is what catches the wings sharp near the top or bottom of the stroke. Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates.
