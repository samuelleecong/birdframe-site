How to batch crop bird photos on a Mac

A day out with a long lens can leave you with a thousand frames where the bird is a small shape in the middle of a lot of branches. Cropping them one at a time takes hours. Here is how to crop the whole set in one go, without losing resolution.

Why cropping in bulk is usually hard

Most batch tools crop every photo the same way: the same box, the same place. That works for product shots, but not for birds, because the bird is somewhere different in every frame. So people end up opening each photo, dragging a crop box around the bird, and moving on to the next one. Some workflows also export a smaller copy, which throws away the detail you paid for in a long lens.

Crop every bird in one run with BirdFrame

BirdFrame is a Mac app made for this one job. It finds the bird in each photo, including small, distant and half-hidden birds, and crops around it.

  1. Choose your photos. An Apple Photos album, or a folder of JPEG, HEIC or RAW files (CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, ORF, RAF, RW2 and DNG). A RAW+JPEG pair counts as one photo.
  2. Pick a framing. Tight, Balanced or Wide, square or rectangular.
  3. Run it. Each bird gets its own crop, so a photo with two birds gives you two crops.
  4. Review and keep. In Photos the crops are reversible edits. From a folder you get new files and the originals are never changed.

Full resolution, nothing generated

Crops are cut from the full-size original. Nothing is downscaled, upscaled or filled in, so every pixel in the crop is one your camera recorded. Everything runs on your Mac and no photos are uploaded.

How long does it take?

In one real run, 17 photos from a single outing gave 20 bird crops in 16 seconds on an M4 Mac. The smallest bird was 0.03% of the frame. Larger batches simply run longer; you can leave it going and come back to the results.

What it costs

BirdFrame is free for up to 50 photos per run, with every feature included. To crop bigger batches in one go, it's a one-time $29.99. No subscription. It needs macOS 15 or later; Apple silicon is fastest.
