The short version

BirdFrame is a Mac app that finds hard-to-see birds in your photos and crops each one in the framing you want: Tight, Balanced or Wide. It works on Apple Photos albums, where crops are reversible edits, and on folders of JPEG, PNG, HEIC or RAW files (CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, ORF, RAF, RW2, DNG), where it writes new files and leaves the originals untouched. Everything runs on-device. It's free for up to 50 photos per run; a one-time $29.99 purchase removes the limit. macOS 15 or later.

Why it exists

A friend of mine was spending hours cropping thousands of bird photos and squinting at them to decide which to keep. I built BirdFrame to help him. Batch cropping made that review much faster, so I turned it into an app for everyone who comes home with a card full of small birds.

Data

How small is the bird? I measured the 4,511 birds BirdFrame found in 1,995 public-domain iNaturalist photos: in the typical photo the main bird fills 6.6% of the frame. Charts and a free CSV.

Free tools

Four free tools that run in the browser on any computer. The online cropper (also in Japanese and Traditional Chinese) finds the birds in one photo and crops each one Tight, Balanced or Wide, without uploading anything; its downloads are up to 1,000 px. The reach calculator shows how many pixels a bird will cover for a camera, lens and distance. The Burst Picker finds the frame with the sharpest bird in a burst, without uploading anything. The bird-in-flight shutter speed calculator shows how much the wings blur at a shutter speed and how often a burst catches them sharp.
