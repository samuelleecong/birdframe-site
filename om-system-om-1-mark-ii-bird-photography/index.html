OM System OM-1 Mark II for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the OM System OM-1 Mark II puts on a bird, worked out from its sensor and the lenses people pair with it: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: the OM System OM-1 Mark II records 5,184 × 3,888 pixels (20.2 megapixels) on a Micro Four Thirds sensor of 17.4 × 13.0 mm, so each pixel is 3.4 µm across. With a 400 mm lens that puts about 1,163 pixels across an American robin at 25 m: crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 57% of the width. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

The tools list the same sensor size and pixel count (17.4 × 13.0 mm, 5,184 × 3,888 pixels) for the OM System OM-1, OM System OM-3, OM System OM-5, Olympus OM-D E-M1X, Olympus OM-D E-M1 Mark III and Olympus OM-D E-M1 Mark II, so every figure here holds for those bodies too. OM System's product page gives the sensor as 17.3 mm wide and its manual as 17.4 mm; these figures use 17.4, and the difference moves them by under 1%.

Why the OM-1 Mark II's small pixels matter

On the same lens, reach comes down to how many pixels fit in each millimetre of sensor. The OM-1 Mark II packs 298 pixels into each millimetre of its 17.4 mm-wide Micro Four Thirds sensor; the full-frame Canon EOS R6 Mark II has 167. On the same lens, at the same distance, the OM-1 Mark II puts 1.78 times as many pixels across the bird: 1,163 pixels across an American robin at 25 m with 400 mm, against 652 on the R6 Mark II.

The wings slow to a stop at the top and bottom of each stroke, which is why a burst catches sharp wingtips that a single frame usually misses.

Questions

How many pixels does the OM System OM-1 Mark II put on a bird?

It depends on the lens and the distance. With 400 mm, about 1,163 pixels across an American robin at 25 m and 577 at 50 m; with 600 mm, 1,758 and 868. The sum: the bird's length × focal length ÷ (distance − focal length), divided by the pixel pitch (3.4 µm).

How far can you crop an OM-1 Mark II photo for iNaturalist or a contest?

A horizontal crop has to keep 40% of the frame's width to fill iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels, 58% for Audubon's 3,000-pixel entries and 68% for Australian Geographic's 3,500-pixel finalist files (2026 rules). Crop from the full-size original and never enlarge a crop to reach a size.

Does the OM-1 Mark II's Micro Four Thirds sensor give more reach than a full-frame camera?

Yes, against a 24-megapixel full-frame body: the OM-1 Mark II has 298 pixels per millimetre of sensor and the Canon EOS R6 Mark II 167, so on the same lens and distance the OM-1 Mark II puts 1.78 times as many pixels across the bird. The 45-megapixel Nikon Z8 has 230, so the OM-1 Mark II's lead over it is 1.30 times.

What shutter speed freezes a bird in flight with the OM-1 Mark II?

With an osprey at about 59 m on 400 mm, wings across two thirds of the frame, the wingtips blur about 15 pixels at 1/2000 and 7 at 1/4000 mid-stroke when you pan with the bird. Holding them within 3 pixels would take about 1/10000, so a burst is what catches the wings sharp near the top or bottom of the stroke. Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates.
