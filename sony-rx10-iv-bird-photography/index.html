Sony RX10 IV for bird photography: the numbers, worked out

What the Sony RX10 IV puts on a bird, worked out from its sensor and its built-in zoom: pixels across birds from chickadee to heron at 10, 25 and 50 m, how they compare with a 600 mm lens on a bigger sensor, how far you can crop and still meet iNaturalist and contest sizes, and how much the wingtips blur in flight.

Short answer: the Sony RX10 IV records 5,472 × 3,648 pixels (20.0 megapixels) on a 1-inch-type sensor of 13.2 × 8.8 mm, so each pixel is 2.41 µm across. At full zoom (220 mm, or 600 mm in 35mm terms) that puts about 883 pixels across an American robin at 25 m: crop to iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels and the robin still fills 43% of the width. The 600 mm is in 35mm terms: a Sony A6400 with a real 600 mm lens puts 1,507 pixels across the same robin. Crop from the full-size original, and never upscale a crop to reach a size.

Fewer pixels on the bird than a 600 mm lens on a bigger sensor

The RX10 IV's 600 mm is a 35mm-equivalent figure: at full zoom the lens is really 220 mm. On an American robin at 25 m it puts 883 pixels across the bird, against 1,507 on a Sony A6400 and 1,152 on a Sony A7 IV with the FE 200-600mm at 600 mm (same calculator, same robin, same distance). Its pixels are smaller, 2.41 µm against 3.92 µm on the A6400, but a real 600 mm lens draws the bird about 2.8 times as large on the sensor.

It also gathers less light. At full zoom the RX10 IV's lens is 220 mm at f/4 (Sony's spec sheet), so its opening is 220 ÷ 4 = 55 mm across; the FE 200-600mm at 600 mm f/6.3 opens to 95 mm, so the RX10 IV collects about a third as much light from the bird; at the same shutter speed, with both shown at the same size, the bird looks noisier. Even with a perfect lens, its 883 pixels across the robin hold less detail than the A6400's 1,507.

Detail here means the finest pattern each camera records at half contrast (MTF50) with a perfect lens, worked out from diffraction in green light (550 nm) at the stated f-number and the pixel size: about 301 line pairs across the robin for the RX10 IV and 523 for the A6400 with 600 mm at f/6.3. Real lenses record less, and both of these are zooms at full stretch.

Birds in flight: wingtip blur at 1/2000 and 1/4000

Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates (est.), so treat these as close guides rather than exact figures. Holding the osprey's wingtips within 3 pixels at this distance would take about 1/12800; for the bald eagle, about 1/10000. Sony's spec sheet gives the RX10 IV's mechanical shutter a top speed of 1/2000 only when stopped down to f/8 or beyond (1/1000 wide open), and its electronic shutter goes to 1/32000, so at full zoom wide open (f/4) both columns need the electronic shutter. The wings slow to a stop at the top and bottom of each stroke, which is why a burst catches sharp wingtips that a single frame usually misses.

Questions

How many pixels does the Sony RX10 IV put on a bird?

It depends on the zoom and the distance. At full zoom (600 mm in 35mm terms, 220 mm real), about 883 pixels across an American robin at 25 m and 440 at 50 m; at 400 mm equivalent, 587 and 293. The sum: the bird's length × real focal length ÷ (distance − focal length), divided by the pixel pitch (2.41 µm).

How far can you crop an RX10 IV photo for iNaturalist or a contest?

A horizontal crop has to keep 37% of the frame's width to fill iNaturalist's 2,048 pixels, 55% for Audubon's 3,000-pixel entries and 64% for Australian Geographic's 3,500-pixel finalist files (2026 rules). Crop from the full-size original and never enlarge a crop to reach a size.

Does the RX10 IV's 600 mm match a 600 mm lens?

No. It's 600 mm in 35mm terms, 220 mm real. At full zoom it puts about 883 pixels across an American robin at 25 m, against 1,507 on a Sony A6400 and 1,152 on a Sony A7 IV with a 600 mm lens, and its 55 mm opening collects about a third the light of a 600 mm f/6.3 lens's 95 mm.

What shutter speed freezes a bird in flight with the RX10 IV?

With an osprey at about 43 m at full zoom, wings across two thirds of the frame, the wingtips blur about 15 pixels at 1/2000 mid-stroke when you pan with the bird. Holding them within 3 pixels would take about 1/12800; at full zoom wide open, anything faster than 1/1000 needs the RX10 IV's electronic shutter (to 1/32000 in Sony's spec sheet), and a burst catches the wings sharp near the top or bottom of the stroke. Wingbeat rates for the osprey and bald eagle are estimates.
